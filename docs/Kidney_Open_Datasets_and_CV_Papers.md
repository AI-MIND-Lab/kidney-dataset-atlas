# Kidney Dataset Atlas: Open Datasets and Computer-Vision Papers

**Web research date:** 22 September 2026; corrections and verified downstream papers added 23 September 2026  
**Purpose:** A linked inventory for the Kidney Dataset Atlas data dictionary.  
**Coverage:** Kidney imaging and pathology, plus broader imaging datasets with kidney annotations. International scope; not limited to Florida or the United States.

> **Status: versioned research backlog.** Nothing in this document is published automatically. Import one record at a time only after its claims, access route, relationships, and eligibility evidence have been verified and a curator has approved it. Preview images remain excluded until reuse permission is explicitly approved. Citation counts have been removed because this backlog does not preserve a source URL for each count.

This is a broad, source-checked starting inventory, **not a verified list of every kidney dataset or every paper**. It includes kidney-specific collections, broader multi-organ collections with verified renal content, derived/aggregate resources, and candidates held separately because access or release details need checking. Entries include versions and derived subsets, so these are **not independent patient cohorts**. The document also contains a research map of influential kidney computer-vision papers and a review-paper map. A final resource section identifies non-image data relevant to a wider kidney dictionary.

## How to read the tables

- **Benchmark:** The dataset's own paper or challenge report evaluates image-analysis methods. This is actual computer-vision use, but is not independent downstream adoption.
- **Downstream:** A separate study reports using the dataset for model development or evaluation.
- **Descriptor:** A publication describes a dataset. Its presence alone does not establish computer-vision experiments.
- **Public release:** A primary repository, archive, or publication identifies publicly released data. This research checked documentation and paper–dataset relationships; it did not download every archive, test every account workflow, or inspect all image metadata.
- **Account/terms:** A public service may require an account and acceptance of terms. This is different from individual institutional approval; where that distinction could not be established, the entry is held in the candidate table.
- **Literature influence:** Papers are included for research relevance, not ranked by citation count. Add a count only when its citation index, exact source URL, and checked date can be stored together.

The intended scope is publicly released, deidentified data that do not require obtaining PHI or controlled clinical-record access. Do not label a dataset “HIPAA-free” based on its name or a public URL. Record the publisher's deidentification statement, exact data license, and access conditions for the release selected. Download permission and permission to reproduce sample images on the website are separate fields.

### 6.3 What the literature analysis shows

1. **Research influence and dataset openness are separate variables.** Several influential kidney-pathology and transplant studies use private institutional cohorts.
2. Public reuse is concentrated around a few families: **KiTS**, **TCGA renal pathology**, the **CT Kidney Normal–Cyst–Tumor–Stone** collection, **HuBMAP/HPA**, and general abdominal resources such as **TotalSegmentator**, **AMOS**, **BTCV**, and **CHAOS**. Automated, unreviewed citation screening (§6.5) found about 90 "strong" use candidates for KiTS19, 140 for the TotalSegmentator dataset, 150 for AMOS, and 120 for CHAOS (not all of them kidney uses). Most kidney-specific releases from 2023 onward had fewer than 10.
3. Dataset counts require a unit: a “sample” may mean a patient, examination, CT volume, series, WSI, patch, or 2D slice. Counts from these levels must never be compared as if equivalent.
4. A paper can use an open dataset together with private external validation. Store access at the **dataset/cohort level**, not as one blanket status for the entire paper.

### 7.1 Gap that motivates the Kidney Dataset Atlas

The search did **not** identify one maintained resource that simultaneously records:

- exact dataset release and parent/derived relationships;
- modality, disease scope, species, and sample granularity;
- direct-open, account/terms, registration-gated, controlled, partial, private, or unclear access;
- license, deidentification evidence, PHI/controlled-data boundary, and sample-image reuse permission;
- papers that trained, validated, tested, or externally validated on the release;
- subject-level versus image-level splitting and potential leakage;
- documented errors, corrections, duplicates, outliers, and annotation provenance.

This is the main contribution opportunity for the proposed website: connect **paper → exact dataset version/subset → access and license → sample unit → split → known quality findings** rather than merely listing papers or reporting model accuracy.

## 8. Notes that should carry into the website dictionary

### Dataset identity and overlap

1. Give each **dataset family, release, modality, and derived annotation set** its own identifier. Relate them using `version_of`, `subset_of`, `derived_from`, or `overlaps_with`.
2. Preserve known relationships: KiTS editions overlap; C4KC-KiTS is the TCIA-hosted KiTS19 training release; RCC-AID derives annotations from TCGA-KIRC, TCGA-KIRP, and TCGA-KICH radiology; ULS23 incorporates processed KiTS21 and DeepLesion components; M3D-Data and SA-Med3D-140K aggregate upstream datasets; AbdomenCT-1K incorporates source datasets (including KiTS19), and the FLARE22 labeled release reuses MSD images with AbdomenCT-1K labels; AbdomenAtlas releases form a versioned aggregation family; HPA images contribute to HuBMAP/HPA challenge subsets; RAOS documents overlap with WORD; M3D-Seg includes KiPA22, KiTS19, and KiTS23. Do not add their sample counts as if all subjects were distinct.
3. Keep TCGA pathology and TCIA radiology separate. A paper using one does not prove use of the other, and not every subject has every modality.
4. Track subjects, scans, series, slides, patches, and 2D images as different units. Use `not reported` when subject counts or patient IDs are unavailable.

### Paper relationships

Use one record per dataset–paper relationship, with the actual role: training, validation, test, external validation, challenge evaluation, or descriptor only. Store a methods-section URL or locator. A reference-list citation, a shared author, or a GitHub star is not evidence of dataset use.

The tables deliberately include original benchmark papers and representative downstream papers. They are not citation-count rankings or complete bibliographies. Missing DOI verification is handled by supplying a verified arXiv, publisher, or author-project link; dataset DOIs must not be presented as paper DOIs. Preprints should retain a preprint label until a journal or conference version is verified.

### Data quality and example images

- Add **documented errors** only when there is a source. For example, the [KPIs data page](https://sites.google.com/view/kpis2024/data) documents corrected alignment and mask-format problems; [WORD](https://github.com/HiLab-git/WORD) documents corrections to publication tables. A paper-table correction is not automatically an error in the distributed images.
- Separate publisher-reported issues from measured findings and possible risks. For slice-level CT classification, missing patient-level split evidence is a limitation to investigate, not proof that leakage occurred.
- For each displayed preview, record its source file, dataset release, modality, label meanings, caption, attribution, and image redistribution permission. Link to publisher-hosted examples until that permission is established.
- Do not use simulated illustrations as if they were real dataset samples. Mark synthetic data, AI-generated annotations, and model predictions explicitly.

### Suggested fields for importing this inventory

| Entity | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| Dataset | `dataset_id`, `name`, `family_id`, `version`, `species`, `modality`, `kidney_scope`, `source_url`, `download_url`, `dataset_doi`, `access_type`, `data_license`, `deidentification_source`, `last_checked` |
| Paper | `paper_id`, `title`, `authors`, `year`, `publication_status`, `doi`, `arxiv_id`, `paper_url` |
| Dataset–paper relationship | `dataset_id`, `paper_id`, `relationship_type`, `subset`, `task`, `evidence_url`, `evidence_locator`, `verification_status` |
| Quality finding | `dataset_id`, `release`, `issue_type`, `description`, `evidence_url`, `reported_or_measured`, `resolution_status` |
| Preview asset | `dataset_id`, `release`, `source_file`, `caption`, `attribution`, `license`, `redistribution_verified` |

### Access-status vocabulary

Use controlled values rather than a single `open: true/false` field:

- `direct_open_download`
- `public_account_or_terms`
- `registration_gated`
- `application_or_manual_approval`
- `controlled_data_use_agreement`
- `partial_or_sample_release`
- `private_institutional`
- `access_unclear`

## 9. Search boundaries and remaining work

Primary sources prioritized in this review were organizer repositories, original research papers, NCI GDC/TCIA/IDC, HuBMAP/HPA, Zenodo, university-hosted pages, and original uploader listings. Some challenge and archive pages could not be retrieved by the web tool; those failures do not establish that a dataset is unavailable to a person using a browser.

Coverage is strongest for renal CT segmentation, TCGA renal pathology, HuBMAP glomerular segmentation, and abdominal multi-organ segmentation. The 22 September 2026 verification pass additionally checked KiTS23, UCSF RMaC, TRUSTED, KiPA22, AbdomenAtlas 3.0, AMOS-MM, TotalSegmentator, SA-Med3D-140K, the TCGA/CPTAC renal collections, RCC-AID, the axial kidney-stone release, ULS23, 3DLAND, DeepLesion, M3D-Data, Merlin, RadImageNet-VQA, and three chest-only controls.

The 23 September 2026 pass re-verified every link, DOI, arXiv identifier, and PMCID against Crossref, DataCite, arXiv, Europe PMC, Zenodo, Figshare, and Hugging Face. It corrected the following:
- the DeepLesion DOI and its annotation provenance (M16);
- the CHAOS repository link (M04);
- per-edition KiTS data licences (K01–K03);
- the KiPA22 access route (H03);
- the RadImageNet-VQA gating (H11);
- publication status for papers that have since been published (MELBA, *Medical Image Analysis*, *Scientific Data*, LNCS, NeurIPS, ICCV, *IEEE TNNLS*, and *The Lancet Digital Health*);
- current release sizes for TotalSegmentator v3.0.0.

It added §6.4: 90 verified dataset–paper rows covering 75 distinct papers. The TCIA and GDC pages for TCGA-KIRP/KICH radiology, the Kaggle listings, and the Synapse terms (BTCV, KPIs) still need a person to check them in a browser.

Further searches are needed for independently released renal MRI disease cohorts, kidney-stone endoscopy, transplant histology, pediatric kidney imaging, and spatial microscopy. Unattributed Kaggle/Roboflow mirrors, private clinical cohorts, code-only releases, and datasets requiring controlled clinical-record access should not be silently promoted into the open catalogue.

**Publication rule for the site:** Preserve the verification and access labels in this file. Do not convert a literature lead, an untested account workflow, or a dataset descriptor into a claim that the full image data are unrestricted and have confirmed independent CV use.
